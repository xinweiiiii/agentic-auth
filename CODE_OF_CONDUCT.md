# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be straight with people, and assume they are being straight with you.

This project is about authorization — a domain where being wrong has consequences for
people who never chose to use it. That makes precision a kindness and vagueness a cost.
Say what you mean, say what you are unsure about, and change your mind in public when the
evidence says to.

## Expected

- **Engage with the argument, not the person.** "This misses the replay case" is useful.
  "You clearly don't understand tokens" is not.
- **Be specific.** A review that names the failing input helps. One that says "this feels
  wrong" asks the author to guess.
- **Say when you don't know.** Confident wrongness costs more time here than admitted
  uncertainty.
- **Assume competence.** Someone who did something unusual may have had a reason. Ask.
- **Accept that maintainers say no.** Scope is a real constraint, and a declined pull
  request is not a judgement of you.

## Not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks.
- Discrimination or demeaning comments about who someone is.
- Sexual language or imagery.
- Publishing someone's private information.
- Sustained disruption of discussion.
- Reporting a vulnerability publicly to pressure a faster fix. See [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

## Scope

Applies in the repository — issues, pull requests, reviews, commits — and anywhere someone
is representing the project.

## Reporting

Email **help@authzai.dev**. Reports go to the maintainers and stay confidential. Include
what happened, where, and when. You will get an acknowledgement within 3 working days.

Maintainers will edit, hide, or delete content that breaks this, and may block accounts
for repeated or severe violations. We will say why.

If a maintainer is the problem, say so in the report. It will be handled by someone else
where that is possible, and disclosed where it is not.
